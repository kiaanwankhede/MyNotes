# RAG explained: why, and what the parts are
> lead: A model cannot know your documents. RAG looks the right pages up first and answers from them. This chapter shows the two stages, the ways basic RAG fails, and the eight parts you choose between.

## Why it matters

Companies keep rules in PDFs. A general chatbot has never seen them, so it either refuses or guesses. RAG (retrieval-augmented generation) is the open-book exam: fetch the relevant documents first, then answer from them. The answer is only as good as what is retrieved.

:::key Prerequisites
Read **Python and Agent Foundations** chapter 2 first (embeddings, context window, hallucination). The examples come from Meridian, a fictional procurement app; its full tour is the last chapter of **Agentic AI with LangChain**.
:::

## Core ideas

### The two stages

the model cannot know your documents; RAG is look up, then answer. Two stages: index once (read, chunk, embed, store), then per question (embed the question, find the closest chunks, prompt, answer). Keyword search misses what meaning search finds (for "who has to approve a very expensive order" they share no useful word). A "meaning map" shows 22 sentences as dots. Eight things go wrong, and a confident wrong answer usually means the right chunk was not retrieved.

### Types of RAG, and when basic RAG hurts

basic RAG hurts when the words differ, the answer is split across chunks, or the right chunk is not first. Improve it **before** the search (change the question), **during** (change what is searched or how), **after** (clean the results) or **beyond** it. The site compares **thirteen variants** (I saw these among them: multi-query, contextual compression, HyDE, query decomposition, follow-up rewrite, hybrid search and reranking) and names four bigger ideas, of which I saw three: corrective RAG grades the retrieved chunks and tries again if they are poor, agentic RAG lets an agent decide whether, what and how often to search, and graph RAG works from a graph of the documents. Which first: right chunk found but not first, try reranking; users phrase things differently, try multi-query or HyDE; several-part questions, decomposition. Meridian uses three: similarity, multiquery and contextual (first-run times 7.1, 12.0 and 9.7 seconds).

### The eight parts, and choosing tools

a RAG system has **eight parts** (parse, chunk, embed, store, retrieve, rerank, generate, evaluate), each a decision. For embeddings check dimensions (768 here), languages, input length, hosted or open, and the rule that **one model must index and search**. Choosing a vector database: how many vectors, do you already run Postgres or Elasticsearch, filtering, managed or self-run, cost.

## Interview corner

:::qa What is RAG?
Find the right pages first, then answer from them: an open-book exam. Chunks of your documents are embedded, stored in a vector database and retrieved by meaning, then given to the model with the question.
:::

:::qa How does it stop the model making things up?
The prompt says to answer from the retrieved context and to say "I do not know" otherwise.
:::
